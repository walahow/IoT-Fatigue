Helmet PPG Trace

Twenty seconds of unfiltered ADC from the analog pulse sensor, captured with a finger on the pad. The sensor plainly responds to skin contact — but nothing in this signal repeats at a heart rate.

No stable periodicity

The sensor is alive. The heartbeat is not in the data.

Peak-to-peak swing rises from 262 counts with no finger to 854 counts with one, so the optical path and the ADC both work. But autocorrelation across the whole 30–180 bpm band never exceeds r = 0.126, and the strongest lag lands at 182 bpm — not a plausible resting rate.

The firmware's beat detector reported 20 beats spread over 42–120 bpm inside a single 15-second window. A resting adult heart does not do that. Those are threshold crossings on noise, so heart rate should be treated as unvalidated until the trace below shows repeating pulse shapes.

What this rules out

  • A dead sensor. Contact changes p2p by 3.3×.
  • A dead ADC pin. Baseline sits at 1945 counts ≈ 1.57 V, a sane mid-rail bias.
  • A sample-rate problem. 100 Hz is ~60× the 1–1.7 Hz band of interest.

What to try next

  • Mechanical coupling first. Steady, light, unchanging pressure; the sensor must not slide.
  • Shield from ambient light — an unshielded photodiode picks up room-light flicker.
  • Re-run the dump and look for r > 0.5 before touching the detector thresholds.
