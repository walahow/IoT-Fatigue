Contact held on all 552 rows — the first session with no dropouts. Rate climbs for three minutes, then declines steadily from 93 to 76 bpm, a least-squares trend of −1.59 bpm per minute. Beat-to-beat variation averages 3.2 bpm, so this is a smooth signal rather than noise.

Read it carefully, though: settling after sitting down looks the same as early drowsiness over nine minutes, and head movement rose 44% over the same period — the opposite of what drowsiness predicts. With no KSS label on this session there is nothing to resolve it against.

Fatigue score · discard

The 44% CRITICAL reading is measuring a sensor fault.

The fuzzy model takes blink rate as an input. This session's blink channel sat at 0.00 for 83% of rows and never exceeded 1/min, because the corneal glint the detector depends on was absent in 97.8% of frames — the eye was not lit brightly enough to throw a reflection.

A rate near zero reads to the model as "almost never blinking", which is a strong drowsiness cue. Rows scoring at or above 70% risk carry a mean blink rate of 0.11. So risk_pct and alert_level describe the dead channel, not the rider, and should be dropped from this session rather than merely discounted.
