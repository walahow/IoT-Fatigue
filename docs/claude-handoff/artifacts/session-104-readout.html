Ground truth · unlabeled distinction

Blink rate is the only channel that separates the phases — and it's the one channel this rider's glasses call into question.

The rider self-reported three blocks: Normal (0–10 min, "no complaints"), Mulai lelah (10–20 min, "neck starting to ache"), Lelah (20–32 min, "yawning, heavy eyes"). Blink rate climbs across them — 0.18 → 0.37 → 0.46 blinks/min — the largest, cleanest-looking effect in the whole session.

But the on-device detector finds blinks from a corneal glint, and glasses are a textbook failure mode: frame edges occlude the eye and lens glare mimics or masks the glint the detector is looking for. A rising blink count recorded through glasses is at least as likely to be worsening glare over the ride as it is drowsiness — there's no way to tell which from this channel alone.

Pulse + IMU · negligible to small

Take blink rate off the table, and nothing moves.

Comparing only Normal against Lelah on the channels that don't depend on the eye — heart rate, pitch, head movement, gyro variance — every effect size (Cohen's d) lands below 0.3, i.e. negligible to small by the usual convention (0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large). The direction is the same story as the chart: motion goes down as the reported fatigue phase progresses, not up. That's the opposite of a "nodding off" signal, and heart rate doesn't move enough to call it a physiological trend either. One session and one rider can't separate a real calming effect from the ride itself changing (route, traffic, fatigue with the neck simply going still) — but as far as this data goes, pulse and IMU alone do not distinguish this rider's three reported phases.

Usable from this session

  • Ground truth exists — a 3-phase self-report label, the first of the three sessions to have one.
  • IMU + pulse both recorded cleanly — 89.5% pulse contact, no dropouts on pitch/gyro/accel.
  • The direction is consistent — every motion channel moves the same way, which rules out a one-off sensor glitch.

Before trusting this label

  • Fix blink detection for glasses. Glint-based detection cannot separate glare from drowsiness on this rider; needed before blink rate means anything here.
  • Record the actual start time. 16:55 is the rider's estimate, not a machine timestamp — noted in the session's own assumptions sheet.
  • Get a second labeled rider. One session can't tell fatigue-driven stillness apart from route conditions changing over the ride.
