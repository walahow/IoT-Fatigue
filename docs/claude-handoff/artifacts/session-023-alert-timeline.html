What limits the claim

Three findings materially change how these alerts should be described. Each was verified against the recorded data, not inferred from the firmware alone.

1  Three of the seven fuzzy rules could not fire

In SD-card mode nothing feeds BLINK: lines over serial, so blink_rate held its never-connected default of 13.0 for all 499 rows. At 13.0, blink_Low and blink_High both evaluate to exactly 0, which zeroes every rule depending on them:

R2Drowsiness collapse → CriticalDead all session
R4Blink low, HR not dropped → WarningDead all session
R6Blink high, HR stable → WarningDead all session
R3Head drop / nodding → CriticalOnly Critical path

R2 is the model’s primary drowsiness rule, and it was mathematically incapable of firing. Every Critical state in this session came from R3 alone.

2  Critical required an elevated heart rate

Re-running the fuzzy model offline against the recorded inputs reproduces the logged risk_pct to within 0.52 percentage points mean absolute error at a 64 BPM baseline, which confirms the reconstruction is faithful. Holding each input at neutral in turn then isolates what drove the output:

  • Neutralise heart rate → Critical seconds fall from 118 to 0.
  • Neutralise nod score → 118 to 72. Neutralise pitch → 118 to 103.
  • Neutralise gyro → Critical rises to 243, because gyro stability is what holds the Safe rules up.

Critical therefore needed head-drop evidence from R3 and an elevated heart rate suppressing the Safe rules. Mean HR on Critical seconds was 83 BPM against the 64 BPM baseline — about +30%, fully inside hr_Elevated. Elevated HR indicates exertion or poor sensor contact; classic drowsiness shows heart rate falling, which is what the dead R2 rule existed to catch.

3  A third of the session had no live IMU

166 of 499 rows have all six IMU axes and head_movement written as exactly zero — the old firmware’s way of signalling a failed read. Those rows collapse pitch_deg to near zero, which zeroes R3, so 150 of the 166 read as Safe and none reached Critical. Across that third of the session the device under-reported rather than over-reported. Excluding those rows raises mean head_movement from 19.6 to 29.4 and mean pitch_deg from 13.6 to 20.3.

What to hand over

Give the 14 sustained episodes, not the raw 58. State the debounce rule applied (merge <5 s, drop <3 s) so the number is reproducible.

Say plainly that these are not validated drowsiness detections. With R2, R4 and R6 dead, the session exercised roughly half the rule base, and Critical reduces to “head pitched forward while heart rate was elevated”. That is a defensible description of what the device did. “The system detected fatigue at 0:43” is not.

Offer the corrected re-run. Camera data for this session now exists — 305 timestamped blinks across 10,710 frames — so a real rolling blinks/min can be substituted and the model re-run offline with all seven rules live. Device output versus corrected output is a stronger result than either number alone.
